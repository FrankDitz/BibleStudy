# Catholic Learning Editorial Guide

**Audience:** One owner in the United States  
**Default tradition:** Latin Church / Roman Rite  
**Status:** Phase 0 editorial policy  
**Last reviewed:** 2026-10-11

## Purpose and boundaries

Learning explains Catholic belief, worship, prayer, seasons, and common practices in plain language. It is a formation aid, not a substitute for the Church, a parish, sacramental preparation, spiritual direction, canon-law advice, medical care, or a confessor.

Content must distinguish doctrine from discipline, liturgy from devotion, universal norms from local practice, and requirements from recommendations. It must never invent a rite, present one local custom as universal, tell the owner that a sacrament has occurred, assess culpability, or claim to resolve a pastoral situation that belongs with a priest or competent Church authority.

## Editorial structure

Use the Catechism's four-pillar structure as the top-level curriculum:

1. **Creed — what the Church believes:** Trinity, creation, Christ, the Holy Spirit, the Church, Mary and the saints, resurrection, and eternal life.
2. **Sacraments — how the Church celebrates:** the Mass, Baptism, Confirmation, Eucharist, Reconciliation, Anointing of the Sick, Holy Orders, Matrimony, sacramentals, funerals, and liturgical space/signs.
3. **Christian life — how Catholics live:** Beatitudes, Commandments, virtues, conscience, works of mercy, fasting/abstinence, vocation, stewardship, and social teaching.
4. **Prayer — how Catholics pray:** the Lord's Prayer, Scripture prayer, Rosary, Liturgy of the Hours, lectio divina, examination of conscience, novenas, adoration, and common devotional prayers.

Cross-cutting collections cover the liturgical year, “what to expect” walkthroughs, glossary, and practical parish participation.

## Source hierarchy

Use the highest applicable primary authority. Lower levels may explain or localize but may not contradict or silently replace a higher one.

| Rank | Source class | Use |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 1 | Sacred Scripture in an approved, licensed Catholic edition | Biblical basis and linked reading; preserve edition attribution |
| 2 | Universal Church primary sources: Catechism, Code of Canon Law, approved liturgical books/instructions, ecumenical councils, papal and dicastery documents | Universal doctrine, sacramental theology, and universal norms |
| 3 | USCCB and approved U.S. adaptations/calendars | United States disciplines, holy days, fasting/abstinence, liturgical adaptations, and current national guidance |
| 4 | The owner's diocese/eparchy | Local holy-day handling, sacramental preparation, dispensations, diocesan policies, and calendar particulars |
| 5 | The owner's parish | Schedules, registration, customary logistics, and whom to contact |
| 6 | Reviewed secondary Catholic sources | Plain-language explanation or historical context only; never the sole authority for a normative claim |

Blogs, social posts, search snippets, AI output, and unattributed handouts are discovery leads, not publishable authorities. Wikipedia may help locate a primary source but is not cited as authority. Dynamic facts such as holy days, fast rules, Mass times, and diocesan policies must be checked at publication and on their review date.

## Jurisdiction and tradition model

Every article carries one of these scopes:

- `universal`: applies across the Catholic Church unless the article names a legitimate ritual distinction;
- `latin-roman`: Latin Church / Roman Rite teaching or practice;
- `national-us`: approved or prescribed for dioceses of the United States;
- `diocesan`: tied to a named diocese and effective date;
- `parish`: tied to a named parish and effective date;
- `devotional`: a legitimate optional devotion, not a liturgical or canonical obligation; or
- `historical-context`: explanatory background with no present-day directive.

The visible article header must render the scope in human terms, for example “United States practice” or “Optional devotion.” Local articles link upward to the universal principle and name the local authority. Eastern Catholic practice is not generalized from Roman-Rite material; an Eastern article requires a source and reviewer competent for that Church.

## Article record and provenance

Each publishable item must have:

- stable content ID, title, summary, collection, and estimated reading time;
- jurisdiction scope, Church/rite, country, and optional diocese/parish identifiers;
- the primary-source URL plus document title and paragraph, canon, section, or page locator;
- separately identified secondary sources, if any;
- `sourceCheckedAt`, `reviewedAt`, `reviewBy`, and content version;
- reviewer name/role recorded outside the public article when privacy requires it;
- liturgical-calendar year or effective date for time-sensitive material;
- required translation/copyright attribution and reproduction limits;
- linked Scripture references stored as canonical app references, not copied provider text;
- a clear “contact your parish/priest” route when individual circumstances matter; and
- a correction note and superseded-version history when a substantive claim changes.

Articles fail closed when a required source, scope, review, or rights field is missing. The app may retain an expired article locally for migration/audit, but it must not present stale directives as current.

## Writing standard

- Lead with what the practice means, then explain what the person will see or do.
- Use “the Church teaches,” “U.S. dioceses observe,” or “many Catholics choose” only when the source and scope support that exact level of certainty.
- Label obligation, recommendation, option, custom, and personal discipline distinctly.
- Explain unfamiliar terms on first use; preserve precise terms such as Eucharist, sacrament, sacramental, solemnity, feast, and memorial.
- Describe the ordinary form of a rite without giving the owner words/actions reserved to ordained ministers.
- Use respectful, non-shaming language. Formation may invite examination and repentance but does not score holiness or gamify reception of sacraments.
- Summarize copyrighted source material and link to it. Use only short quotations when necessary and permitted.
- Do not fabricate quotations, citations, indulgence conditions, promises attached to devotions, or private revelations.
- When legitimate practices differ, state the variants and their jurisdictions instead of forcing a single answer.

## Reviewer role and workflow

The owner writes or commissions a draft; the app does not self-publish generated religious instruction.

1. **Source check:** an editor verifies every material claim against the recorded primary source and confirms the source is current.
2. **Jurisdiction check:** the editor marks universal, Roman, national, diocesan, parish, devotional, or historical scope.
3. **Theological/liturgical review:** a qualified Catholic reviewer confirms fidelity, terminology, pastoral framing, and the distinction between norms and customs. Sacramental and liturgical walkthroughs should be reviewed by a priest, deacon, trained catechist, or liturgy professional appropriate to the topic.
4. **Rights check:** an editor verifies quotation, prayer text, image, calendar, and Scripture-edition permissions.
5. **Product QA:** test links, Scripture routes, accessibility, dark mode, localization expansion, and offline/stale behavior.
6. **Approval:** record reviewer role, dates, source snapshot identifiers, and next review date before publishing.
7. **Maintenance:** re-review on the scheduled date or immediately after a relevant Church, national, diocesan, parish, or source change.

AI may help outline, simplify, compare drafts, or flag missing citations. Its output remains untrusted until every claim completes this workflow.

## Review cadence

| Content | Maximum routine interval | Immediate re-review triggers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Core doctrine and stable prayer explanations | 24 months | New authoritative text, correction, or reviewer concern |
| Liturgical/sacramental walkthroughs | 12 months | New missal/ritual edition, adaptation, decree, or local change |
| U.S. calendar, holy days, fast/abstinence | Each liturgical year | USCCB or diocesan update/dispensation |
| Diocesan/parish directions | 90 days | Source-page change, office notice, or broken link |
| External links and contact details | 90 days | Redirect, failure, or domain ownership change |

## Initial curriculum acceptance list

Phase 5 content planning begins with these reviewed modules:

- the shape of the Mass, participation, postures, reception guidance, and what to expect as a visitor;
- the seven sacraments, their purpose, ordinary preparation, minister, and who to contact;
- Reconciliation: preparation and order of the rite without replacing a confessor or judging culpability;
- the liturgical year: Advent, Christmas, Lent, Triduum, Easter, Ordinary Time, solemnities, feasts, memorials, and U.S. holy days;
- fasting and abstinence with U.S. and diocesan scope labels;
- Rosary prayers and mysteries, lectio divina, adoration, examination of conscience, and Liturgy of the Hours;
- sacred space, vessels, vestments, gestures, sacramentals, funerals, blessings, and common Catholic vocabulary;
- Creed, Beatitudes, Commandments, theological/cardinal virtues, gifts/fruits of the Holy Spirit, and works of mercy; and
- “ask your parish” guides for becoming Catholic, sacramental preparation, marriage, sickness, death, and local ministries.

Completion means the owner can understand and participate more confidently; it does not mean the app certifies catechetical formation or sacramental readiness.

## Safety and pastoral escalation

Direct the owner to a priest/parish/diocese for sacramental eligibility, validity, dispensations, annulments, canonical penalties, serious conscience questions, or instructions that differ locally. Direct emergencies to local emergency services and appropriate professionals; the app is not pastoral crisis care. Never delay urgent medical help in favor of a devotional practice or sacrament-planning flow.
