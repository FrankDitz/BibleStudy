# Virtue Curriculum Review Packet

**Curriculum status:** progressive manuscript rewrite; qualified Catholic review pending
**Content baseline:** 2026-10-14
**Current reviewable scope:** eleven-lesson foundation course and all four ten-lesson cardinal courses: Prudence, Justice, Fortitude, and Temperance. Seven capital-sin counter-virtue courses retain their planned structure but remain unavailable prototypes pending full manuscripts.

## Purpose

The Virtues tab is a guided library for learning what virtue is, seeing it in Scripture, and applying it in ordinary life. It is not a moral score, an examination of conscience, a substitute for Confession or spiritual direction, or a claim that lesson completion proves growth in holiness.

## Source method

1. Scripture, the *Catechism of the Catholic Church*, ecumenical-council teaching, and current universal-Church teaching govern doctrine and pastoral framing.
2. Joseph Rickaby's *Four-Square; or, The Cardinal Virtues* is a historical secondary guide for the orientation and cardinal courses only. It never governs doctrine and is not a source for the seven counter-virtue courses.
3. Scripture encounters use references in the Catholic canon and open in the app's licensed Bible reader; the curriculum does not reproduce unlicensed translation text.
4. Pope Francis's 2024 general-audience catecheses on the virtues provide current pastoral language and practical emphasis.
5. Every lesson names its sources and remains visibly marked “Catholic review pending” until approved by a qualified reviewer.

The shared technical gate requires at least 800 words of instruction and 1,000 authored words overall in every available lesson, three Scripture passages totaling at least 24 verses, eight distinct sections, five review prompts, full formation supports, and a time estimate derived from the real material. Passing that gate establishes depth and structure; it does not constitute theological approval.

Temperance is one virtue encountered through two pedagogical lenses, not two different virtues. The gluttony course focuses on appetite and consumption; the cardinal course studies ordered pleasure and use of created goods more broadly.

## Historical-source guardrails

Rickaby's 1908 work is treated as a valuable historical source, not as the sole or final authority. The adaptation preserves his strongest durable insights:

- virtue is a stable readiness shaped through repeated free acts;
- prudence is right reason in action, not fearfulness;
- justice gives what is due, and charity does not excuse injustice;
- fortitude governs fear and rashness and is often shown through patient endurance;
- temperance orders desire without condemning created goods;
- humility belongs with truthful magnanimity;
- acquired habits and the infused life of grace must be distinguished and held together.

The app omits dated rhetoric, stereotypes, culture-bound examples, and claims that require a contemporary doctrinal or pastoral qualification. Where Rickaby's framing and current sources differ in emphasis, the Catechism and current magisterial teaching govern the lesson.

## Reviewer checklist

- [ ] Definitions agree with the Catechism and do not overstate contested theological opinions.
- [ ] Scripture references are contextually appropriate and use the Catholic canon.
- [ ] Historical adaptations accurately represent Rickaby without importing obsolete rhetoric.
- [ ] The distinction between human effort and grace is clear.
- [ ] Prudence is not presented as anxiety; fortitude is not presented as aggression; temperance is not presented as contempt for the body.
- [ ] Justice and charity are related without allowing one to cancel the other.
- [ ] Chastity language is pastoral, age-appropriate for the owner, and consistent with Catholic teaching.
- [ ] Reflection prompts avoid scrupulosity, diagnosis, coercion, and claims to measure holiness.
- [ ] Completion language records study activity only.
- [ ] Source locators and links are accurate.
